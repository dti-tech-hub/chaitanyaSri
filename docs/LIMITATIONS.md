# Limitations and threats to validity

## 1. Scope limitation (most important)

The signal controller is a **software simulation**. It is not connected to a
real traffic light, does not control traffic, and has not been validated on a
public road. No result in this repository should be presented as a real-world
safety, emissions, response-time or congestion benefit.

## 2. Dataset limitations

- **Fire engines are scarce**: 132 train, 35 val and 20 test boxes. Per-class
  fire-engine metrics have wide uncertainty and may not generalise.
- The two source datasets come from different cameras, countries and annotation
  conventions. Domain shift is expected on a new city or camera.
- The test split is small (143 images). Small differences in precision or mAP
  should not be treated as meaningful without confidence intervals or repeated
  splits, which were not available.
- `normal_vehicle` is broad and comes from one source. It is not a complete road
  user taxonomy (no pedestrians, cyclists, buses or trucks as separate classes).
- Labels are boxes, not tracks or segmentation masks. Tracking quality is not
  measured against a MOT ground truth.
- The test set contains no dedicated negative images (scenes without emergency
  vehicles), so an image-level ROC/AUC may be unavailable. If it is available,
  it is auxiliary and not a detection metric.
- The Open Images subset was excluded because its downloaded annotations
  contained only ambulance boxes. Including it would have introduced false
  negatives for the other classes.

## 3. Detector limitations

- YOLOv8n at 448 px is a small CPU-friendly model. It is less accurate than
  larger models, especially on small, distant, occluded or unusual vehicles.
- Confidence is not calibrated. A score of 0.60 is a decision threshold, not a
  probability that the vehicle is truly an ambulance.
- The model can confuse liveries (e.g. a white van with markings, a police
  vehicle with unusual colours) or miss emergency vehicles at night or in rain.
- The detector was trained on still images; video-specific artifacts (motion
  blur, compression, rolling shutter) are only partially represented.
- The held-out test split is from the same source distribution as training, so
  it does not establish cross-city generalisation.

## 4. Tracker limitations

- The default IoU tracker is simple and deterministic. It can swap IDs when
  vehicles overlap or when detections flicker.
- ByteTrack/BoT-SORT were not used because the optional `lap` package could not
  be installed. This is a missing comparison, not a claim that IoU is better.
- There is no video-level track annotation, so MOTA/IDF1 are not reported.
- Lane assignment smooths over five frames; a vehicle changing lanes near the
  stop line can be assigned late or not at all. The fail-safe is to withhold
  priority, which is safe but can miss a legitimate EV.

## 5. Controller and simulation limitations

- The simulation is signal-level. It models arrivals, green onsets and a fixed
  crossing/hold time. It does not model queue spillback, saturation flow,
  pedestrians, cyclists, buses, emergency-vehicle escort, or driver behaviour.
- Normal vehicles are generated by a Poisson process with fixed rates. Real
  arrivals are correlated and time-varying.
- The simulation uses a fixed 2 s confirmation delay as a proxy for detection
  and tracking confirmation. Real confirmation depends on frame rate, occlusion
  and detector confidence.
- The controller serves one EV at a time. A second EV may wait behind the first;
  this policy is documented but not optimised.
- The baseline is an idealised fixed-time plan with the same green/yellow/all-red
  durations. It is not a field-optimised signal plan, so the comparison should
  not be read as “priority beats the best possible fixed-time control.”
- The event-level results include cases where priority is slower than the
  baseline (an EV arriving on green still pays confirmation and, after a prior
  priority event shifts the cycle, may pay preparation). The mean improvement is
  driven by red-arrival events. This is reported rather than averaged away.
- The controller's 45 s maximum green and 10 s cooldown are prototype choices,
  not traffic-engineering standards.

## 6. Metric limitations

- Waiting time is measured from the first confident lane-assigned detection, not
  from the physical arrival at the stop line. A late first detection reduces the
  measured wait, so detection latency and waiting time are entangled.
- Baseline waiting time is a counterfactual computed from the fixed-time
  schedule, not an observed vehicle in a parallel universe. It is exact for the
  model, not for the real world.
- Throughput is counted at green onset, not at completion of crossing. It is a
  consistent relative measure, not an absolute capacity measurement.
- FPS and latency are from one CPU machine and one session. They are not a
  hardware benchmark.
- mAP at `conf=0.001` is a ranking metric; it is not the same as the operating
  point used by the controller. Both are reported separately.
- No confidence intervals or significance tests are reported because the test
  split and the number of scheduled EVs are small and the simulation is
  deterministic. Means and standard deviations are descriptive.

## 7. Safety and ethical limitations

- A false positive could give priority to a non-emergency vehicle in a real
  deployment. The confirmation logic reduces this risk but does not eliminate
  it; the project does not claim otherwise.
- A false negative could delay a real emergency vehicle. The fail-safe withholds
  priority rather than guessing a lane, which is safe for conflicting traffic
  but not necessarily safe for the emergency response.
- The demo footage contains real vehicles and identifiable locations. It is used
  only for local demonstration and retains its original licence; it is not
  redistributed as training data.
- The project does not process biometric data or personal information. No human
  subjects were recruited.
- Real deployment would require traffic-engineering review, fail-safe hardware,
  cybersecurity assessment, regulatory approval and field testing. None of that
  was done here.

## 8. Threats to validity summary

| Type | Threat | Mitigation / honest statement |
|---|---|---|
| Internal | Test data leakage | Source splits and an image-level screen are reported; a later perceptual-hash audit found 87/143 near-duplicate test frames, so a screened re-evaluation (56 images) is reported alongside the full split |
| Internal | Baseline chosen to lose | Same controller durations and same arrival times; event-level results shown |
| Construct | mAP is not deployment accuracy | Operating-point FP/FN also reported; fire scarcity stated |
| External | Single-city dataset | Domain shift acknowledged; no cross-city claim |
| External | Schematic lane geometry | Calibration tool provided; demo geometry labelled schematic |
| Statistical | Small test set / five EVs | Descriptive statistics only; no p-values or broad claims |
| Ecological | Signal simulation is not real traffic | Explicitly labelled simulated; no safety/emissions claims |
